### [62] [sft-wick: A formalism and package for Feynman-diagram expansion and evaluation in stochastic field theories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19480)
*Zheng Zhang*

Main category: physics.comp-ph

TL;DR: This paper presents sft-wick, an open-source Python package that constructs Feynman diagram tables (topologies, coefficients, signs, causal constraints) for stochastic field theories with multi-component fields, tensor couplings, and non-Gaussian noise, then numerically evaluates the diagram integrals without brute-force Wick contraction.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Stochastic field dynamics in path-integral formulation lead to combinatorially explosive perturbation theory due to factorial Wick pairings, tensor-index routing, and causal constraints, making manual or brute-force automated expansion intractable.

Method: The package enumerates spatial topologies before routing component indices (avoiding contraction-by-contraction Wick expansion), derives algebraic coefficients, enforces response-field constraints (vanishing response-response contractions, Itô prescription, no causal response loops), and evaluates integrals from user-supplied response/cumulant functions.

Result: Predictions from sft-wick agree with direct Langevin simulations to within the simulation's statistical noise.

Conclusion: sft-wick provides a systematic, scalable, and validated tool for automated Feynman diagram computation in stochastic field theories, avoiding combinatorial explosion by topology-first enumeration and handling tensor-valued couplings and non-Gaussian noise.

Abstract: When stochastic field dynamics are cast into a path-integral formulation, perturbation theory becomes systematic but the resulting expansion quickly grows combinatorially large. The setting targeted here includes multi-component, multi-dimensional fields with matrix propagators, tensor-valued couplings, and non-Gaussian driving noise specified by arbitrary $n$-point cumulants. Wick pairings grow factorially, and component indices must be routed through the tensor-valued vertices. The useful output is not a raw contraction list, but a diagram table: one entry per topology, with multiplicities, coupling sums, signs, and causal constraints resolved. We present sft-wick, an open-source Python package that constructs these diagram tables and computes their integrals numerically. Given an action and an observable, it enumerates topologically distinct Feynman diagrams, derives their algebraic coefficients, and evaluates the resulting diagram integrals from user-supplied response and cumulant functions. The core algorithm enumerates spatial topologies before routing component indices, avoiding contraction-by-contraction Wick expansion. Response-field constraints, including vanishing response-response contractions, the ito prescription, and the absence of causal response loops, are enforced during enumeration. Predictions are validated against direct Langevin simulation, agreeing to within the simulation's statistical noise.

### [63] [TorchNEP: Ultra-Efficient and Accurate Training of Neuroevolution Potentials](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19557)
*Yong-Chao Wu,Xiaoya Chang,Tero Mäkinen,Amin Esfandiarpour,Jian-Li Shao,Tapio Ala-Nissila,Zheyong Fan,Mikko Alava*

Main category: physics.comp-ph

TL;DR: TorchNEP is a PyTorch-based reimplementation of the Neuroevolution Potential (NEP) framework that accelerates training by over 100x through analytically derived gradients, adaptive optimization, and a two-stage training strategy, while revealing that the two-stage protocol (not the optimizer) is primarily responsible for accuracy improvements.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The original NEP training strategy is computationally expensive, which limits systematic exploration of model architectures and training protocols for large-scale atomistic simulations.

Method: TorchNEP implements NEP in PyTorch with (1) analytically derived gradients, (2) adaptive optimization, and (3) a two-stage training strategy, achieving >100x speedup while maintaining full backward compatibility.

Result: Training accelerated by over two orders of magnitude; two-stage training (not optimizer choice) is the key driver of accuracy improvement; consistent improvements in force and stress predictions across diverse benchmarks; increased model complexity reduces training errors but does not necessarily improve predictive performance.

Conclusion: TorchNEP provides an efficient and flexible training framework for developing more accurate and robust machine-learned interatomic potentials.

Abstract: Neuroevolution Potential (NEP) is one of the most efficient machine-learned interatomic potential frameworks for large-scale atomistic simulations. However, its original training strategy remains computationally demanding, limiting systematic exploration of model architectures and training protocols. Here, we present TorchNEP, a PyTorch-based implementation of NEP that combines analytically derived gradients, adaptive optimization, and a two-stage training strategy. TorchNEP accelerates training by more than two orders of magnitude while maintaining full compatibility with existing NEP models. We further show that the improvement in predictive accuracy primarily originates from the two-stage training protocol rather than the optimization algorithm itself. Across diverse benchmark datasets, TorchNEP consistently improves force and stress predictions while maintaining comparable or improved energy accuracy. Benchmark evaluations on elemental and alloy systems demonstrate enhanced predictive performance for both atomic configurations and key materials properties. Furthermore, we show that increasing model complexity does not necessarily improve predictive performance despite reducing training errors. Overall, TorchNEP provides an efficient and flexible training framework for developing more accurate and robust machine-learned interatomic potentials.

### [64] [Machine-learned prediction of carbon interstitial clusters in diamond](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19600)
*Xiaoya Chang,Arsalan Hashemi,Nima Ghafari Cherati,Mikko Karttunen,Ádám Gali,Tapio Ala-Nissila*

Main category: physics.comp-ph

TL;DR: This paper uses active learning to build a carbon interstitial dataset, benchmarks three ML interatomic potentials (GAP, NEP, MACE) against DFT, finds MACE most accurate, and uses validated potentials in annealing MD to discover previously unreported carbon interstitial clusters (di- to octa-interstitials) with in-gap states relevant to quantum technologies.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Carbon self-interstitials in diamond compete with optically active point defects used in quantum technologies, yet their configurational landscape is poorly understood due to subtle energy differences governing competing minima and pathways.

Method: Active learning to build an interstitial-focused dataset; benchmarking of three ML interatomic potentials (GAP, NEP, MACE) against DFT for energies, forces, and migration barriers; annealing molecular dynamics using the validated potentials.

Result: MACE accurately reproduces DFT energetics and relative stabilities, while GAP and NEP can misorder ground states. Annealing MD reveals previously unreported carbon interstitial clusters (di- to octa-interstitials), several introducing in-gap states of interest as colour centres, with metastability governed by kinetically accessible pathways rather than energetic ordering.

Conclusion: The study charts the carbon interstitial defect landscape in diamond and accelerates defect discovery for quantum technologies.

Abstract: Diamond hosts optically active point defects central to quantum technologies, yet the carbon self-interstitials introduced during growth and irradiation compete with them and form new defects whose configurational landscape is poorly charted, as subtle energy differences govern the competing minima and pathways. Here we build an interstitial-focused dataset by active learning and benchmark three machine-learning interatomic potentials -- GAP, NEP and the equivariant MACE -- against density functional theory for energies, forces and migration barriers. MACE reproduces the reference energetics and relative stabilities, whereas the others can misorder the ground states. Annealing molecular dynamics with the validated potentials uncovers a series of previously unreported carbon interstitial clusters, from di- to octa-interstitials -- several introducing in-gap states of interest as colour centres -- and shows that their metastability is governed by kinetically accessible pathways rather than energetic ordering. These results chart the interstitial defect landscape and accelerate defect discovery for quantum technologies.

### [65] [The Heat Kernel Expansion: Curvature for Shock Detection in Higher-Order Financial Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19860)
*Mohammad Elsayed,Sara Najem*

Main category: physics.comp-ph

TL;DR: This paper analyzes Norwegian financial networks over nine years using simplicial complexes, finding that curvature (derived from heat kernel expansion) is a more sensitive measure than Euler characteristic, Betti numbers, or torsion for detecting regulatory shocks in board interlock networks.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To determine which topological and geometrical measures can reliably detect external shocks (e.g., legislative changes) in evolving financial networks, specifically board director-company affiliations modeled as simplicial complexes.

Method: Financial networks (directors as nodes, companies as faces) are modeled as simplicial complexes. Three topological measures are computed: Euler characteristic (via Betti numbers), torsion (via reduced determinant of higher-order Laplacians), and higher-order clustering coefficients. Additionally, curvature is introduced as a geometrical measure derived from coefficients of the heat kernel's series expansion in powers of time.

Result: Euler characteristic and torsion fail to capture the effect of imposed legislation on board representation. Curvature successfully detects shocks: inflection points correspond to external forcing, minima to shock arrival times. Torsion components show sharp transitions; higher-order clustering shows smooth changes. The number of spanning trees can change at lowest order without affecting torsion.

Conclusion: Curvature derived from heat kernel expansion is a sensitive and reliable measure for detecting shocks in financial networks, unlike traditional topological measures like Euler characteristic or torsion which lose local information or fail to reflect structural changes.

Abstract: This work follows the evolution of financial networks in Norway over a period of nine years at a monthly rate. The data consist of board directors and their affiliations to companies, which we model as simplicial complexes. In this framework, directors are represented as nodes and companies as faces of the complex. To characterize the latter, we focus on three topological measures: the Euler characteristic, computed through the Betti numbers, torsion computed through the reduced determinant of the higher-order Laplacians, and higher-order clustering coefficients. The first two fail to capture the effect of imposed law on representation, unlike our notion of curvature which is a geometrical measure computed from the coefficients of the series expansion of the heat kernel in powers of time, which is our major contribution in this work. In particular, the Euler characteristic integrates curvature, and thus local information is lost. Subsequently, not every topological measure can reliably capture shocks in networks. Further, the number of spanning trees may undergo significant changes at the lowest order, yet these changes need not be reflected in the torsion. Conversely, the change in the curvature revealed variation in the board interlock due to legislation, and serves as a sensitive measure for detecting shocks in networks. Inflection points in curvature are associated with external forcing, and minima with shock arrival times. Sharp transitions are also observed in the components of torsion, while smooth changes are observed in higher-order clustering.

Main category: physics.comp-ph

<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To validate a threshold inception-based multiphysics modeling approach for ionic wind fans by direct comparison with experimental measurements, and to identify limitations and refinement needs for more accurate engineering-oriented simulation tools.

Method: A wire-to-cylinder electroaerodynamic (EAD) fan with variable electrode spacing was used as a reference system. Numerical simulations employing a threshold inception-based multiphysics model were compared against experimental measurements of airflow characteristics, discharge current, and performance trends under atmospheric conditions. Experimental characterization of emitter wire surface morphology was conducted, and surface roughness was incorporated into the model.

Result: Numerical simulations showed good qualitative agreement with experiments; systematic deviations emerged at higher voltages and larger electrode gaps. The commonly assumed perfectly smooth emitter surfaces limit model accuracy. Micro-scale surface protrusions on the emitter wire locally enhance the electric field and alter corona inception. Incorporating representative surface roughness improved quantitative agreement with measured airflow velocities.

Conclusion: The threshold inception model provides a robust foundation for EAD fan simulations, but electrode surface morphology is a critical factor for reliable prediction. This work advances validation and refinement of ionic wind fan modeling methodologies and identifies key considerations for developing more accurate simulation tools.

Abstract: This study investigates the predictive capability of a threshold inception-based multiphysics modeling approach for ionic wind fans by direct comparison with experimental measurements. Analysis of these discrepancies indicates that the commonly adopted assumption of perfectly smooth emitter surfaces can limit model accuracy. Experimental characterization of the emitter wire reveals micro-scale surface protrusions, which locally enhance the electric field and alter corona inception behavior. Incorporating representative surface roughness into the numerical model improves quantitative agreement with measured airflow velocities. The results demonstrate that while the threshold inception model provides a robust foundation for EAD fan simulations, electrode surface morphology is a critical factor for reliable prediction. This work advances the validation and refinement of ionic wind fan modeling methodologies and identifies key considerations for the development of more accurate engineering-oriented simulation tools.

### [67] [A Social Force Model of the Evacuation from a Big Box Store](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20136)
*Gavin A. Buxton*

Main category: physics.comp-ph

TL;DR: An anisotropic social force model is enhanced with elliptical cross-sections for pedestrians, irregular polygons for wheelchair users, velocity/angular dependence for collision avoidance, overlap-based physical interactions, decision-making, social groups, panic spread, and herding behavior. Simulating a box store evacuation shows that ignoring 'staff only' exits significantly increases average egress times.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To improve the realism of pedestrian evacuation modeling by incorporating physical diversity (wheelchair users), behavioral nuances (anisotropic avoidance, social groups, panic, herding), and decision-making, in order to better understand the impact of exit choice and perceived exit availability on evacuation efficiency.

Method: An anisotropic social force model is extended with elliptical/polygonal body shapes, overlap-area-based physical interaction forces, velocity-dependent avoidance, decision-making algorithms, social group dynamics, panic propagation, and herding mechanisms. An evacuation scenario in a large box store is simulated with shortest-path navigation to exits.

Result: Ignoring 'staff only' exits and relying solely on main entrances can significantly increase average egress times during evacuation.

Conclusion: Perceived exit availability (e.g., ignoring staff-only exits) substantially impacts evacuation efficiency, highlighting the need for models that capture realistic behavioral and physical heterogeneity in crowd dynamics.

Abstract: We include elliptical cross-sections to physically represent people, and irregular polygons to represent wheelchair users, in an anisotropic social force model whose velocity and angular dependence also captures the social tendency for people to avoid walking into one another. Physical interactions are included that depend on the area of overlap between people, or obstacles, to capture normal forces that resist compression and tangential forces that resist sliding motion. The model is further extended to include decision making capabilities, small social groups, the spread of panic, and herding behavior. A large box store is simulated during an evacuation where people move through the store, along the shortest path, to their desired exits. The effects of exit choice, or the perceived availability of exits, on exit times is elucidated. It is found that ignoring 'staff only' exits, and only exiting from the main entrances, can significantly increase average egress times.
