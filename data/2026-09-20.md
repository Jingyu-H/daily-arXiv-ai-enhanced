*Oliver T. Schmidt*

Main category: physics.comp-ph

<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Standard parametric reduced-order models (e.g., POD-GPR) struggle to represent moving sharp features such as shocks, because the localized discontinuity is not efficiently captured by a low-dimensional linear subspace built under a POD-energy criterion. The work aims to improve accuracy of shock-dominated fields without resorting to direct filtering of the pressure field.

Method: Each detected shock location is treated as a random variable with a prescribed probability density; averaging over this artificial distribution replaces the localized pressure change with a smooth transition whose spatial extent is set by the density width. Each snapshot is decomposed exactly into a regularized mollified field and a local sharp correction that restores the shock. Implementation choices include: a calibrated indicator for shock detection; a compactly supported Wendland kernel for mollification; a peak-normalized (and, where necessary, partitioned) weight derived from each shock-location density that defines the centroid, principal axes, and scales of the local alignment domain; and separate POD-GPR models for the mollified field and aligned corrections, with additional regressions for shock presence and alignment.

Result: On the transonic airfoil pressure data of Catalani et al. (2023), compared with a standard POD-GPR model built from the same data and POD-energy criterion, the mollified-sharp model reduces the mean test-set relative L2 pressure error by 31.2% and the mean test-set surface-pressure-coefficient error by 33.2%, while also improving predicted shock locations and pressure changes. The trade-off is a median online evaluation time 2.24 times as long.

Conclusion: The probabilistic mollified-sharp decomposition provides a general, modular framework for parametric reduced-order modeling of moving sharp features, delivering substantial accuracy gains at the cost of increased online evaluation time, and is in principle applicable to other parameter-dependent fields with moving sharp features, such as moving material interfaces in multiphase flows.
