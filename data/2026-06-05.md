### [1] [Hydrogen-induced lattice cohesion weakening favors atomic displacement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05325)
*Liang Gao,Yiran Mao,Markus Wilde,Xiaoou Yi,Cong Li,Shiwei Wang,Thomas Schwarz-Selinger,Jan Coenen,Richard Kembleton,Sebastijan Brezinsek,Christian Linsmeier,Guanghong Lu*

Main category: cond-mat.mtrl-sci

TL;DR: Lattice-dissolved hydrogen weakens interatomic cohesion in metals, facilitating atomic displacement and dislocation motion at sub-threshold stresses, providing a mechanism for hydrogen-enhanced localized plasticity and hydrogen embrittlement, verified experimentally on plasma-exposed metal surfaces.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To uncover the atomic-scale mechanism by which lattice-dissolved hydrogen (LDH) weakens lattice cohesion, enabling atomic displacement and dislocation movement under sub-threshold stress, and to explain hydrogen-enhanced localized plasticity in hydrogen embrittlement.

Method: Theoretical identification of LDH's role in weakening interatomic cohesion, combined with quantitative experimental verification using low-energy hydrogen plasma exposure on metal surfaces, where defect generation occurs without sufficient ion momentum for direct displacement damage.

Result: Hydrogen-induced lattice cohesion weakening is quantitatively verified; LDH independently reduces cohesion strength, facilitating defect generation and plasticity even below direct displacement thresholds.

Conclusion: The cohesion-weakening effect of LDH, distinct from defect-trapped hydrogen, provides a new paradigm for understanding hydrogen embrittlement through enhanced atomic displacement and dislocation mobility.

Abstract: Atomic displacement -- the fundamental process underlying diverse deformation and damage phenomena in metals, from irradiation defect production to stress-driven dislocation motion -- is governed by interatomic cohesion strength. Here, lattice-dissolved hydrogen (LDH) occurring in metals under direct hydrogen exposure is identified to effectively weaken lattice cohesion, and thereby facilitating atomic displacement and dislocation movement upon plastic deformation in sub-threshold stress regime. This atomic-scale insight provides a physically transparent mechanism for hydrogen-enhanced localized plasticity implicated in hydrogen embrittlement. We quantitatively verify the hydrogen-induced lattice cohesion weakening effect on metal surfaces exposed to low-energy hydrogen plasma, where massive defects are generated despite the absence of sufficient ion momentum for direct displacement damage. By unprecedentedly quantifying the cohesion-weakening effect of LDH independently from defect-trapped H, we establish a new paradigm to understand hydrogen embrittlement.

### [2] [Breakdown of Fluctuational Electrodynamics in the Extreme Near Field](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05845)
*Philippe Ben-Abdallah*

Main category: cond-mat.mes-hall

TL;DR: This paper shows that the standard assumption of statistically independent thermal fluctuations between bodies breaks down at extreme near-field distances due to hybridization of optical phonons across nanometric gaps, deriving a correction to radiative heat flux using a coupled-oscillator model and Green-tensor formalism.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To demonstrate that the conventional fluctuational-electrodynamics assumption of statistically independent thermal fluctuations between distinct bodies fails in the extreme near-field regime, and to quantify the resulting correction to radiative heat transfer.

Method: A microscopic coupled-oscillator model combined with a Green-tensor formulation of the Poynting vector to derive correlation-induced corrections to the radiative heat flux.

Result: Correlations between fluctuating currents at opposite interfaces become significant when the hybridization energy is comparable to the intrinsic damping rate, substantially modifying conventional predictions at subnanometric separations for polar materials supporting surface phonon-polaritons.

Conclusion: Establishes a microscopic framework for correlated thermal fluctuations in the extreme near-field regime and quantifies their impact on radiative heat transfer.

Abstract: Fluctuational electrodynamics relies on the assumption that thermal fluctuations in distinct bodies are statistically independent. We show that this approximation breaks down in the extreme near-field regime, where overlapping evanescent surface fields hybridize optical phonons across nanometric vacuum gaps and generate fluctuating-current cross correlations between opposite interfaces. Using a microscopic coupled-oscillator model combined with a Green-tensor formulation of the Poynting vector, we derive the resulting correlation-induced correction to the radiative heat flux. For polar materials supporting surface phonon-polaritons, these correlations become significant when the hybridization energy is comparable to the intrinsic damping rate and can substantially modify conventional fluctuational-electrodynamics predictions at subnanometric separations. Our results establish a microscopic framework for correlated thermal fluctuations in the extreme near-field regime and quantify their impact on radiative heat transfer.

<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To investigate whether widely available generative AI (ChatGPT) can produce synthetic experimental data that appears scientifically meaningful to experts, focusing on quantum electronic device measurements, and to assess the ethical implications for data integrity in peer-reviewed research.

Method: Using ChatGPT to generate synthetic data mimicking well-known quantum device experiments (qubit control, Majorana fermions, Josephson effects, quantum dots, quantum wires) based solely on knowledge of physics equations and basic features of experimental signals, without training on specialized datasets. Also tests AI's ability to augment real data and mimic instrument noise.

Result: AI can generate realistic, dramatic signals for iconic quantum effects using basic mathematical models. AI can augment real data and mimic common scientific instrument noise. The approach succeeds without needing specialized training data.

Conclusion: Sharing large volumes of primary experimental data is recommended to prevent undisclosed synthetic data proliferation, as consistently generating long measured sequences poses sufficient barriers for AI to mimic convincingly.

Abstract: With the advance of generative artificial intelligence (AI) synthetic texts and images have become commonplace. These capabilities offer clear benefits, but have also raised a number of ethical concerns that often have to do with misrepresenting AI outputs as genuine material. A lesser known capability of generative AI is to perform the basic analysis, processing and even synthesis of numerical data. This raises the question of whether AI can be used to imitate experimental data that an expert would consider scientifically meaningful and on par with data in the figures of peer-reviewed manuscripts? In this paper, we focus on synthesizing data inspired by well-known experiments done frequently on quantum electronic devices. This field is related to information technologies such as spintronics and quantum computing, and is considered data-rich and data-driven. We demonstrate that it is possible to generate dramatic signals associated with iconic effects such as quantum bit control, Majorana fermions, Josephson effects, quantum dots and wires using widely available ChatGPT. We find that because some of the clearest data from quantum devices can be expressed in terms of relatively basic mathematical models, AI does not need to learn on the specialized body of data. Instead, knowledge of the physics equations and of the basic features of experimental signals can go a long way towards building a realistic dataset. We also demonstrate that real data can be augmented by AI, and that AI can mimic the noise of common scientific instruments. To help assure that published data come from experiments and are not synthesized by AI, we recommend sharing large volumes of the primary data. While it is straightforward for AI to mimic a few sets of data, consistently generating long measured sequences poses sufficient barriers to the proliferation of undisclosed synthetic data.

Abstract: Non-reciprocal supercurrents enable non-dissipative rectification, holding great promise for superconducting electronics. Conventionally, this non-reciprocity, termed the superconducting diode effect, requires the simultaneous breaking of time-reversal and parity symmetries. Here, we propose a superconducting triode effect in an asymmetric quantum-dot Josephson junction coupled to an additional metallic top gate, which breaks the parity symmetry while explicitly preserving time-reversal symmetry. We demonstrate that the supercurrent across this junction exhibits a strong non-reciprocal effect that can be continuously manipulated via the top gate to achieve an ideal unidirectional supercurrent, thus manifesting a superconducting triode effect. Furthermore, under radio-frequency radiation, this junction exhibits highly asymmetric Shapiro steps, realizing fully quantized supercurrent rectification. Our work not only provides an alternative physical mechanism for the superconducting diode effect observed in Josephson junctions with explicit time-reversal symmetry, but also introduces a new tuning knob to manipulate supercurrent non-reciprocity.

### [6] [Particle-Hole Ghost Interference in Superconductors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06437)
*Archisman Panigrahi,Vladislav Poliakov,Leonid Levitov*

Main category: cond-mat.supr-con

TL;DR: This paper proposes a boundary-assisted impurity interference effect in superconductors—an electron analogue of mirror-assisted optical interference—where quasiparticle waves scattered by an impurity interfere with those reflected from a line defect, terrace edge, or phase boundary. This generates a "ghost" interference pattern with both Friedel oscillations and hyperbolic fringes, which is parametrically stronger than two-impurity Young's interference and serves as a sensitive local probe of superconducting electronic order via STM/STS.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To develop a robust local probe of superconducting electronic order that overcomes limitations of two-impurity Young's interference (which requires second-order perturbation) by exploiting boundary-assisted single-impurity interference, which is already strong at first order in the impurity potential.

Method: The authors theoretically analyze quasiparticle interference from a single impurity near a line defect, terrace edge, or phase boundary. They consider waves scattered directly from the impurity and waves reflected by the boundary, showing that their interference yields a pattern combining conventional 2k_F Friedel oscillations with additional hyperbolic fringes.

Result: The ghost interference pattern appears at first order in the impurity potential (parametrically stronger than two-impurity Young's interference). The spatial modulation extends beyond λ_F/2 and is directly sensitive to the quasiparticle structure of the paired state, including possible Fermi-surface anisotropy of the superconducting order parameter.

Conclusion: Boundary-assisted impurity interference provides a robust local probe of superconducting electronic order with clear signatures accessible to STM/STS measurements.

Abstract: Mirror-assisted optical interference can improve the fidelity of Young's double-slit interference. Here we discuss an electron analogue: particle-hole interference in superconductors produced by a single impurity near a line defect, terrace edge, or phase boundary. Quasiparticle waves scattered directly from the impurity interfere with waves reflected by the boundary, generating a ``ghost'' interference pattern that combines conventional $2k_F$ Friedel oscillations with additional hyperbolic fringes. Compared to the recently studied two-impurity Young's interference, this effect appears already at first order in the impurity potential and is therefore parametrically stronger. The resulting spatial modulation extends beyond $λ_F/2$ and is directly sensitive to the quasiparticle structure of the paired state, including possible Fermi-surface anisotropy of the superconducting order parameter. These findings point to boundary-assisted impurity interference as a robust local probe of superconducting electronic order, with clear signatures accessible to STM/STS measurements.

### [7] [Polylogarithmic Structure of Bragg Diffraction in Finite-Coherence Lattices](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05886)
*Evangelos G. Filothodoros*

Main category: cond-mat.stat-mech

TL;DR: The paper develops a polylogarithmic framework for Bragg diffraction using a weighted multi-plane interference model, where scattering amplitudes are expressed as polylogarithms. This formulation unifies peak positions, widths, and line shapes from a single analytic structure, naturally capturing deviations from ideal crystals due to finite correlation length, disorder, and non-uniform coherence.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Conventional diffraction models for disordered or partially coherent materials often require additional phenomenological broadening assumptions. The authors aim to provide a compact analytical framework that naturally captures these deviations without extra assumptions.

Method: A polylogarithmic generating function is derived from a weighted multi-plane interference model. The scattering amplitude takes the form $F(θ) = \mathrm{Li}_m\left(e^{iθ_{\mathrm{eff}} - ε}\right)$, where ε is a finite coherence length. The classical Bragg condition corresponds to the polylogarithm argument approaching its branch point z=1.

Result: The model recovers the standard Bragg law for ideal crystals while capturing deviations due to finite correlation length, disorder, and non-uniform lattice coherence. Bragg peaks correspond to boundary singularities of the polylogarithm, establishing a connection between diffraction theory and complex analysis.

Conclusion: This polylogarithmic formulation provides a unified analytic description of diffraction line shapes within a generalized correlation model, particularly relevant for disordered or partially coherent materials where conventional models require additional phenomenological broadening.

Abstract: We develop a polylogarithmic structure for Bragg diffraction based on a weighted multi-plane interference model. Within this kind of construction, the scattering amplitude is expressed as a polylogarithmic generating function. By introducing extra contributions with power-law and the usual exponential decay, it takes the form $F(θ) = \mathrm{Li}_m\left(e^{iθ_{\mathrm{eff}} - ε}\right)$, where $ε$ is a finite coherence length. In the limit where $ε\rightarrow 0$, the argument of the polylogarithm approaches the unit circle and the classical Bragg condition corresponds to the approach of the polylogarithm argument toward its branch point $z=1$. This formulation provides a compact analytical framework for describing diffraction line shapes within a generalized correlation model in which peak positions, widths, and line shapes arise from a single analytic structure. Although we are able to recover the standard Bragg law for ideal crystals, the polylogarithm model captures deviations due to finite correlation length, disorder and non-uniform lattice coherence. We show that if Bragg peaks correspond to boundary singularities of the polylogarithm, a connection between diffraction theory and complex analysis arise. The proposed theoretical model may be particularly relevant for disordered or partially coherent materials, where conventional diffraction models often require additional phenomenological broadening assumptions.

</details>
