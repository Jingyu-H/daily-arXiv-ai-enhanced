*Christopher Kang,Rahul Verma,Aditya Sonpal,Alyson Shoji,Christophe Chipot,Jim Pfaendtner*

Main category: physics.chem-ph

TL;DR: FK-eABF replaces the histogram-based mean-force accumulator of eABF with sparse Gaussian kernels and Nadaraya-Watson regression, achieving faster early-time convergence and smooth free-energy landscapes without sacrificing long-term accuracy across diverse systems spanning four orders of magnitude in simulation time.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To overcome the limitations of conventional eABF's histogram-based mean-force accumulator (e.g., minimum-count thresholds and delayed convergence) by introducing a kernel-based reformulation that provides smooth, accurate free-energy estimates from the earliest stages of simulation.

Method: FK-eABF replaces histogram binning with sparse Gaussian kernels storing local running-mean forces; biasing forces are recovered via Nadaraya-Watson regression, and an auxiliary self-attenuating exploration force is defined by the same kernel population without needing prior barrier height knowledge.

Result: FK-eABF achieves full free-energy landscape coverage faster than WT-MetaD, OPES, and WTM-eABF on N-acetyl-N'-methylalanylamide in water; recovers near-isoenergetic balance of Abl1 kinase DFG-in/out transition in multi-microsecond simulations; and recovers the free-energy landscape of 1,3-butadiene electrocyclic ring closure within 30 ps at ab initio MD level.

Conclusion: FK-eABF is more than a kernelized implementation of eABF—it is a force-based kernel reformulation that delivers faster early-time convergence without sacrificing long-time quantitative accuracy, validated across systems spanning over four orders of magnitude in simulation time.

Abstract: We introduce force-kernel extended-system adaptive biasing force (FK-eABF), a force-based kernel reformulation of eABF that replaces the histogram-based mean-force accumulator of conventional eABF with a sparse population of Gaussian kernels storing local running-mean forces. Biasing forces are recovered by Nadaraya-Watson regression, yielding smooth estimates from the earliest stages of a simulation without a minimum-count threshold, while the same kernel population also defines an auxiliary, self-attenuating exploration force that requires no prior knowledge of barrier heights. On N-acetyl-N'-methylalanylamide in explicit water, FK-eABF achieves full free-energy landscape coverage faster than well-tempered metadynamics (WT-MetaD), on-the-fly probability enhanced sampling (OPES), and WTM-eABF, while all four methods converge to comparable accuracy given sufficient time. FK-eABF also retains long-time accuracy: on the DFG-in/out transition of Abl1 kinase, multi-microsecond simulations recover the established near-isoenergetic balance between states. At the opposite extreme, applied to the electrocyclic ring closure of 1,3-butadiene at the ab initio molecular dynamics level, FK-eABF recovers the free-energy landscape within 30 ps. Together, these benchmarks, spanning more than four orders of magnitude in simulation time, establish FK-eABF as more than a kernelized implementation of eABF: A force-based kernel reformulation that delivers faster early-time convergence without sacrificing long-time quantitative accuracy.

### [77] [pANO-F12: An atomic natural orbital-inspired route to more compact basis sets for F12 explicitly correlated methods](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22543)
*Vladimir Fishman,Jan M. L. Martin*

Main category: physics.chem-ph

TL;DR: This work introduces pseudo-Atomic Natural Orbital (pANO) basis sets for explicitly correlated F12 methods, which are more compact than standard cc-pVnZ-F12 basis sets while maintaining comparable accuracy. The pANO-F12 basis sets are derived via energy minimization with linear independence constraints, yielding a familiar shell structure and offering cost-effective alternatives, particularly at double- and triple-zeta levels.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Standard cc-pVnZ-F12 basis sets are substantially larger (e.g., cc-pVDZ-F12 is the size of cc-pVTZ), making them costly for medium-sized systems. The goal is to generate more compact F12 basis sets while preserving the fast convergence property of explicitly correlated methods.

Method: The authors develop "pseudo-ANO" (pANO) basis sets via an energy minimization-based contraction process under linear independence constraints, functionally equivalent to traditional ANO contraction. This recipe is applied to produce pANO-F12 basis sets from the same elemental primitives as cc-pVnZ-F12.

Result: pANO-F12 exhibits the familiar shell structure seen in standard orbital basis sets (unlike cc-pVnZ-F12). It is validated on thermochemical benchmarks and the out-of-plane vibrations of benzene. The approach is most beneficial at double- and triple-zeta levels, offering either superior performance at same cost or comparable performance at lower cost.

Conclusion: pANO-F12 basis sets provide a route to more compact F12 basis sets, especially advantageous for medium-sized systems and when combined with localized pair natural orbital methods.

Abstract: Explicitly correlated methods such as MP2-F12 and CCSD(F12*) exhibit much faster basis set convergence (asymptotically $\propto L^{-7}$, with L the highest angular momentum) than orbital-only approaches. Yet it has been pointed out that cc-pVnZ-F12 basis sets themselves are substantially larger than the corresponding cc-pVnZ, and specifically that cc-pVDZ-F12 is the size of cc-pVTZ. One way to generate compact basis sets in an orbital-only context are Atomic Natural Orbital (ANO) basis sets [J. Almlöf and P. R. Taylor, JCP 86, 4070 (1987)]. However, obtaining the required first-order reduced density matrix while properly accounting for the F12 geminal is problematic. In this work, we show that an energy minimization-based contraction process under linear independence constraints yields `pseudo-ANO' (pANO) basis sets that are functionally equivalent in quality. Subsequently, we apply this recipe to obtain pANO-F12 basis sets from the same elements, then validate them for several thermochemical benchmarks and for the hypersensitive out-of-plane vibrations of benzene. We show that, unlike cc-pVnZ-F12, pANO-F12 exhibits the familiar shell structure seen in cc-pVnZ and ANO basis sets, and that pANO-F12 offers a route to more compact F12 basis sets more amenable to medium-sized systems, especially in conjunction with localized pair natural orbital approaches. Overall, the pANO approach is most beneficial for the smaller double-and triple-zeta basis sets, offering either superior performance to cc-pVnZ-F12 at same cost, or similar performance at lower cost.

### [78] [Machine Learning Interatomic Potentials: Advancing Open-Source Software for Efficient and Scalable Molecular Simulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22698)
*Christoph Brunken,Titouan Cormier,Lucien Walewski,Marco Carobene,Yessine Khanfir,Zachary Weller-Davies,Miguel Bragança,Armand Picard,Adrien Pichard,Leon Wehrhan,Heloise Chomet,Eszter Varga-Umbrich,Marie Bluntzer,Massimo Bortone,Valentin Heyraud,Silvia Acosta-Gutiérrez,Jules Tilly,Olivier Peltre*

Main category: physics.chem-ph

TL;DR: mlip v2 is a new generation of the mlip library that improves MLIP-based molecular simulation through modular API redesign, a high-performance equivariant backend (e3j), new eSEN architecture with Mixture-of-Experts, improved electrostatics modeling, and advanced simulation features like NPT ensembles and NEB methods, all under Apache 2.0 license.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Broader adoption of MLIPs is hindered by fragmented tooling, limited scalability, and inflexible software design, motivating a unified and extensible framework.

Method: The library introduces an API redesign for modularity, an e3j backend for fast equivariant operations, the eSEN architecture with Mixture-of-Experts, physically grounded charge modeling with long-range interactions, and advanced simulation capabilities (NPT, NEB).

Result: Significantly accelerated model inference and simulations, scalable training on large/diverse datasets, and broader applicability to complex, reactive, and out-of-equilibrium systems.

Conclusion: mlip v2 bridges the gap between ML research and practical molecular simulation, enabling efficient modeling of complex systems, and is publicly available on GitHub and PyPI.

Abstract: Machine learning interatomic potentials (MLIPs) enable atomistic simulations with near ab initio accuracy at significantly reduced computational cost, but their broader adoption is often limited by fragmented tooling, limited scalability, and inflexible software design. We present mlip v2, a new generation of the mlip library that advances efficient and scalable molecular simulation through a unified and extensible framework. The new release features a targeted API redesign with improved modularity and control, enabling flexible customization of training, data processing, and simulation workflows. It further integrates a new high-performance backend for equivariant operations, e3j, significantly accelerating model inference and simulations. In addition, the framework introduces a range of entirely new capabilities, including the eSEN architecture with a Mixture-of-Experts formulation for scalable training on large and diverse datasets, improved handling of electrostatics through more physically grounded charge modeling and long-range interaction treatment, and advanced simulation features such as NPT ensembles and nudged elastic band methods. Together, these extensions significantly broaden the scope of MLIP applications, enabling efficient modeling of complex, reactive, and out-of-equilibrium systems, and bridging the gap between ML research and practical molecular simulation applications. The library is available on GitHub and on PyPI under the Apache license 2.0.
