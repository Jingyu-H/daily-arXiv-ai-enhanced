### [1] [Chiral spin liquid and chiral antiferromagnetism in half-filled moiré Hubbard model: possible applications to twisted bilayer TMDs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14503)
*Chuyi Tuo,Hong Yao*

Main category: cond-mat.str-el

TL;DR: Using large-scale DMRG simulations of the minimal moiré Hubbard model on a triangular lattice at half-filling with spin-dependent staggered flux to capture displacement field effects, the paper shows that the displacement field significantly enriches the phase diagram: it destabilizes the chiral spin liquid beyond a narrow weak-field regime, induces chiral correlations in the 120°-antiferromagnetic phase, stabilizes incommensurate spin-density wave phases at weak coupling, and reveals signatures of a continuous chiral spin liquid to chiral antiferromagnetic transition potentially driven by spinon condensation.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Twisted transition metal dichalcogenides provide a highly tunable moiré platform for correlation physics, but the interplay between displacement field and twist angle in these systems remains unresolved. The authors aim to clarify how the displacement field enriches the triangular Hubbard model's phase diagram and guides future experiments.

Method: Large-scale density matrix renormalization group (DMRG) simulations are employed to study the minimal moiré Hubbard model on a triangular lattice at half-filling, with the displacement field effect captured by a spin-dependent staggered flux.

Result: The displacement field destabilizes the chiral spin liquid phase beyond a narrow weak-field regime, induces pronounced chiral correlations in the strong-coupling 120°-antiferromagnetic phase, and stabilizes incommensurate spin-density wave phases at weaker coupling. Signatures of a continuous transition between the chiral spin liquid and chiral antiferromagnetic phases are found at a finite displacement field, potentially driven by spinon condensation.

Conclusion: The displacement field drives rich many-body physics in the triangular moiré Hubbard model, substantially enriching the phase diagram and providing useful guidance for future experiments in moiré superlattice systems.

Abstract: Twisted transition metal dichalcogenides offer an exceptionally tunable moiré platform for studying correlation physics beyond conventional condensed matter systems. In particular, the intriguing interplay between the displacement field and the twist angle remains to be fully resolved. In this paper, we use large-scale density matrix renormalization group simulations to study the minimal moiré Hubbard model on a triangular lattice at half-filling, where the displacement field effect is captured by a spin-dependent staggered flux. We find that the displacement field significantly enriches the triangular Hubbard phase diagram in several qualitative ways. It rapidly destabilizes the chiral spin liquid phase beyond a narrow weak-field regime, induces pronounced chiral correlations in the strong-coupling $120^\circ$-antiferromagnetic phase, and stabilizes incommensurate spin-density wave phases at weaker coupling. We further find signatures of a continuous transition between the chiral spin liquid and chiral antiferromagnetic phases at a finite displacement field, potentially driven by spinon condensation. Our results uncover rich displacement-field-driven many-body physics and provide useful guidance for future experiments in moiré superlattice systems.

### [2] [Strong photoresponse of edge two-dimensional electrons in a magnetic field](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13811)
*Sergey A. Mikhailov,Wladislaw Michailow*

Main category: cond-mat.mes-hall

TL;DR: This paper develops a quantum theory of photocurrent generation in two-dimensional electron gases under out-of-plane magnetic fields, where edge-state electrons reach Fermi-velocity propagation speeds. It predicts that microwave/terahertz photon absorption by these edge electrons enables sensitive radiation detection, and proposes an experimental geometry using radiation confinement structures to achieve high photocurrent efficiency. The photoresponse in III-V semiconductors is predicted to be orders of magnitude stronger than in graphene.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To develop a theoretical framework for photocurrent generation in two-dimensional electron gases with edge states under magnetic fields, and to identify an experimental geometry that enables efficient microwave and terahertz radiation detection with sensitivity exceeding that of graphene-based detectors.

Method: The authors develop a detailed quantum theory of photocurrent generation driven by photon absorption by edge-state electrons in a two-dimensional electron gas subject to an out-of-plane magnetic field. They incorporate radiation confinement structures in the proposed experimental geometry to enhance photocurrent generation efficiency, and compare the predicted photoresponse in III-V semiconductor structures against graphene.

Result: The theory predicts a strong photocurrent generation efficiency can be achieved with suitably arranged radiation confinement structures. The photoresponse in III-V semiconductor structures is shown to be orders of magnitude higher than that measured in graphene.

Conclusion: Edge-state electrons in two-dimensional electron gases under out-of-plane magnetic fields provide an effective pathway for sensitive microwave and terahertz radiation detection, with III-V semiconductor platforms offering significantly superior photoresponse compared to graphene. The proposed experimental geometry with radiation confinement structures enables powerful photocurrent generation.

Abstract: Electrons in two-dimensional electron gases in the presence of an out-of-plane magnetic field propagate along the edge with a high velocity of the order of the Fermi velocity. Under microwave and terahertz radiation, photon absorption by these electrons provides a pathway to realising sensitive radiation detection. Here, we develop a detailed quantum theory of the photocurrent generated in such a system by an incident electromagnetic wave and propose an experimental geometry for observing the predicted phenomenon. By using suitably arranged radiation confinement structures, a strong photocurrent generation efficiency can be obtained. We also demonstrate that the resulting photoresponse in III-V semiconductor structures can be orders of magnitude higher than that measured in graphene.

### [3] [Linear-response time-dependent density-functional theory with local range separation: Core and valence resonances of the neon atom](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14052)
*Jari van Gog,Felipe Zapata,Julien Toulouse*

Main category: physics.chem-ph

TL;DR: The paper investigates range-separated hybrids (RSHs) and locally range-separated hybrids (LRSHs) in linear-response TDDFT Sternheimer calculations of the photoionization spectrum of the Ne atom, testing a new two-parameter range-separation function designed to improve the high-density limit.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The Ne atom photoionization spectrum is a stringent test for approximate exchange-correlation treatments because it exhibits both valence and core resonances with very different energy scales. Previous work used a simple one-parameter local range-separation function; this paper aims to assess a more flexible two-parameter version.

Method: TDDFT Sternheimer calculations of the photoionization spectrum of Ne using RSHs and LRSHs, comparing a two-parameter range-separation function against the earlier one-parameter function.

Result: The LRSH approach with the two-parameter function gives a satisfactory overall description of the photoionization spectrum, including valence and core resonance energies. Valence (2s→np) lifetimes are reasonably reproduced, but core (1s→np) lifetimes are overestimated by orders of magnitude due to Auger decay channels involving double excitations absent in adiabatic, single-determinant TDDFT.

Conclusion: More accurate resonance widths within linear-response range-separated TDDFT would require multideterminant schemes and/or a frequency-dependent response kernel.

Abstract: We investigate range-separated hybrids (RSHs) and locally range-separated hybrids (LRSHs) for linear-response time-dependent density-functional theory (TDDFT) Sternheimer calculations of the photoionization spectrum of the Ne atom. This system constitutes a stringent test for approximate exchange-correlation treatments because it exhibits both valence and core resonances with very different energy scales. Building on previous work employing a simple one-parameter local range-separation function, we assess here a more flexible two-parameter range-separation function designed to improve the high-density limit. We compare photoionization spectra and resonance parameters obtained with RSHs and LRSHs. We find that the LRSH approach with the two-parameter range-separation function provides an overall satisfactory description of the photoionization spectrum, including energies for both valence and core resonances. The lifetimes of the 2s $\rightarrow$ np valence resonances are also reasonably reproduced since their decay does not involve double excitations. In contrast, the lifetimes of the 1s $\rightarrow$ np core resonances are overestimated by orders of magnitude because their Auger decay channels involve double excitations that are absent in adiabatic, single-determinant TDDFT. Obtaining more accurate resonance widths within linear-response range-separated TDDFT would require using multideterminant schemes and/or adding a frequency-dependent response kernel.

</details>

### [4] [Thickness-dependent secondary-electron emission from suspended MoS$_2$ membranes in the helium ion microscope](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13688)
*Cyan Kim,David Lister,Philip Jackle,Karen L. Kavanagh*

Main category: cond-mat.mtrl-sci

TL;DR: This paper correlates secondary-electron (SE) emission intensity in a helium ion microscope with independently measured thickness of suspended nanometric MoS2 membranes, finding a peak response at 40–55 nm thickness with a back-to-front SE ratio up to 4.7. An asymmetric SE-escape model with a ~10 nm exit-side escape depth (vs. 2 nm entrance) reproduces the data, providing a rapid thickness screening method and a test bed for low-energy ion-solid interaction models.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To understand how SE emission in the helium ion microscope depends on membrane thickness in the thin-film regime where He ions can transmit through the sample, and to develop a rapid thickness screening tool for suspended membranes while testing low-energy ion-solid interaction models.

Method: SE intensity from suspended nanometer-thick MoS2 flakes on lacey carbon was measured in a HIM and correlated with thickness determined independently by electron energy-loss spectroscopy (EELS). SRIM ionization profiles were combined with an asymmetric SE-escape model featuring a longer escape depth on the exit side of the membrane to reproduce the observed thickness-dependent response.

Result: The SE response peaks at 40–55 nm membrane thickness, with an apparent back-to-front SE signal ratio reaching 4.7. The peaked, thickness-dependent component is attributed primarily to SE emission at the bottom (exit) surface rather than transmitted ions striking instrument surfaces. The model yields an effective exit-side escape depth of ~10 nm, five times the assumed 2 nm entrance value.

Conclusion: The SE signal correlation provides a rapid thickness screen for suspended membranes and offers a route to testing low-energy ion-solid interaction models in thin materials. The large apparent exit-side escape depth suggests either efficient energy transport to the exit surface or a shifted SRIM energy-deposition profile due to effects such as channeling.

Abstract: Secondary-electron (SE) emission in the helium ion microscope (HIM) becomes sensitive to membrane thickness when the sample is thin enough for He-ion transmission and when the SEs emitted from the bottom surface are collected. We correlated the total SE intensity of suspended, nanometer-thick MoS$_2$ flakes on lacey carbon with thickness measured independently by electron energy-loss spectroscopy. The response peaks at 40-55 nm, with an apparent back-to-front SE signal ratio reaching 4.7. The peaked, thickness-dependent component is attributed primarily to SE emission at the bottom surface of the flake, rather than to transmitted ions striking instrument surfaces. Applying SRIM ionization profiles, an asymmetric SE-escape model with a longer escape depth on the exit side reproduces the response. We find an effective exit-side escape depth of approximately 10 nm, five times the assumed 2 nm entrance value, suggesting that deposited energy reaches the exit surface far more efficiently than the entrance surface or that the SRIM model's energy deposition profile is shifted by an effect such as channeling. The correlation provides a rapid thickness screen for suspended membranes and a route to testing low-energy ion-solid interaction models in thin materials.

</details>

### [5] [Far-from-equilibrium topological phase transition in one dimension](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13658)
*Ze-Min Huang,Gustav John,Sebastian Diehl*

Main category: cond-mat.stat-mech

TL;DR: The paper uncovers a mechanism for far-from-equilibrium topological phase transitions using a one-dimensional compact phase model evolving deterministically from random initial conditions. The transition is driven by competition between vortex-induced disordering and relaxation toward homogeneity, and its universality class is identified as directed percolation (DP), confirmed via effective field theory and numerical dynamical-scaling analysis.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To uncover a mechanism for far-from-equilibrium topological phase transitions that rests on topology and symmetry rather than on phenomenological postulates, providing a first-principles understanding of such transitions.

Method: The authors construct a one-dimensional compact phase model evolving deterministically from random initial conditions. They leverage phase compactness (which permits vortices) and phase-shift symmetry (which implies a homogeneous fixed point that suppresses vortex nucleation), then build an effective field theory and perform numerical dynamical-scaling analysis to confirm DP critical scaling.

Result: The competition between vortex-induced disordering and relaxation toward homogeneity drives a continuous nonequilibrium transition whose universality class is directed percolation (DP). This is confirmed by both constructing the effective field theory and numerically verifying DP critical scaling through dynamical-scaling analysis.

Conclusion: The work establishes a topology- and symmetry-based mechanism for far-from-equilibrium topological phase transitions, identifying directed percolation as the universality class of the resulting continuous transition.

Abstract: We uncover a mechanism for far-from-equilibrium topological phase transitions, via a one-dimensional compact phase model evolving deterministically from random initial conditions. It rests on topology and symmetry rather than on phenomenological postulates: phase compactness permits vortices, and a homogeneous fixed point suppresses their nucleation, with the fixed point itself implied by phase-shift symmetry. The competition between vortex-induced disordering and relaxation toward homogeneity drives a continuous nonequilibrium transition, whose universality class we identify as directed percolation (DP). We demonstrate this by constructing the corresponding effective field theory and numerically confirming DP critical scaling through dynamical-scaling analysis.

</details>

### [6] [From Photothermal Transients to Effective Transport Operators: An Operator-Based Interpretation of TTG Dynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13813)
*Slobodanka Galovic,Steva Jacimovski*

Main category: physics.app-ph

TL;DR: This paper presents a Mori-Zwanzig projection framework-based forward model for transient thermal grating (TTG) measurements, describing heat transport via a non-Markovian linear evolution operator with memory. The framework treats the experimentally observed TTG transient as the response of an effective transport operator, with diffusive and long-memory behaviors emerging as asymptotic limits, providing a systematic route from TTG measurements to effective transport operators and coarse-grained microscopic transport descriptions.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: TTG measurements probe thermal transport at submicrometer spatial and subnanosecond temporal scales where departures from purely diffusive behavior have been reported in various solid-state systems. A systematic theoretical framework is needed to interpret these deviations from diffusive transport and connect them to underlying microscopic transport mechanisms.

Method: The authors formulate a forward model for TTG response within the Mori-Zwanzig projection framework, where heat transport is described by a linear evolution operator with memory. Microscopic transport processes are systematically coarse-grained into a non-Markovian transport operator with finite memory, providing a general description of TTG dynamics. The inversion procedure determines effective operator parameters, with microscopic transport mechanisms inferred through subsequent material-specific modeling.

Result: The framework demonstrates that diffusive and long-memory behaviors emerge as asymptotic limits of the same underlying formalism. The primary outcome is the determination of effective operator parameters from TTG transients, rather than direct identification of microscopic transport mechanisms, which requires a secondary material-specific modeling step.

Conclusion: The proposed Mori-Zwanzig inspired framework establishes a systematic route from TTG measurements to effective transport operators and subsequently to coarse-grained descriptions of microscopic transport dynamics, providing a general and unified perspective on non-diffusive thermal transport across scales.

Abstract: Transient thermal grating (TTG) measurements probe thermal transport on submicrometer spatial and subnanosecond temporal scales, where departures from purely diffusive behavior have been reported in a variety of solid-state systems. Within the Mori-Zwanzig projection framework, such effects can be interpreted as manifestations of memory arising from microscopic degrees of freedom that remain unresolved at the observational scale. Motivated by this perspective, we formulate a forward model for TTG response in which heat transport is described by a linear evolution operator with memory. Microscopic transport processes are systematically coarse-grained into a non-Markovian transport operator, whose finite-memory form provides a general description of TTG dynamics, while diffusive and long-memory behaviors emerge as asymptotic limits of the same underlying framework. Within this formulation, the experimentally observed TTG transient is interpreted as the response of an effective transport operator rather than as a direct signature of a particular microscopic transport mechanism. The primary outcome of the inversion procedure is the determination of effective operator parameters, while microscopic transport mechanisms are inferred only through a subsequent, material-specific modeling step. The proposed Mori-Zwanzig inspired framework therefore establishes a systematic route from TTG measurements to effective transport operators and, subsequently, to coarse-grained descriptions of microscopic transport dynamics.

</details>
