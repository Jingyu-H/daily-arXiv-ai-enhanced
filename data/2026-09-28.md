### [1] [The conductance filtration: sink-relative persistence and exact edge sensitivity for heat transport in particle-filled composites](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30810)
*Daisuke Yasufuku*

Main category: cond-mat.mtrl-sci

TL;DR: A unified graph formulation of particle-pair thermal conductance as three branch types (contact, near-contact, bond), with a conductance filtration and sink-relative persistence; the dissipation share equals the logarithmic sensitivity of effective conductance, enabling exact closed-form prediction of single-edge changes and diagnostics by mechanism.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To unify three distinct thermal transport mechanisms between particles under a single graph representation and to connect dissipation allocation to a sensitivity/persistence framework for diagnostic purposes.

Method: Formulate pairwise thermal conductance g_e as three branch types--contact, near-contact, and bond--within one construction; introduce the conductance filtration using g_e as axis, plus persistence relative to the sink boundary; derive dissipation share as the logarithmic sensitivity of effective conductance and derive an exact closed form for a finite change of a single edge.

Result: Dissipation share is exactly the logarithmic sensitivity of effective conductance; a finite single-edge change is predicted exactly in closed form; allocation of dissipation across branch types and idealisation ratios for interface, matrix, and particle mechanisms are obtained as diagnostics on the same graph.

Conclusion: A single graph-based conductance filtration with sink-relative persistence yields exact sensitivity identities and closed-form single-edge predictions, providing unified diagnostic quantities (dissipation allocation and mechanism idealisation ratios) across contact, near-contact, and bond transport.

Abstract: We formulate the thermal conductance $g_e$ of a particle pair as three branch types -- contact, near-contact and bond -- within a single construction, and we introduce the filtration that takes this $g_e$ as its axis (the conductance filtration) together with persistence taken relative to the sink boundary. We then show that the dissipation share is exactly the logarithmic sensitivity of the effective conductance, and that a finite change of a single edge is predicted exactly in closed form. The allocation of the dissipation among branch types, and the idealisation ratio of each mechanism (interface, matrix and particle), are thereby obtained as diagnostic quantities on one and the same graph representation.
